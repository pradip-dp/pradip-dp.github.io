Winning Best Paper at DVCon India: A Journey Through GenAI-Driven Formal Verification

Reflecting on our groundbreaking research that earned Best Paper at DVCon India 2024, and what it means for the future of hardware verification.

When we first started exploring the intersection of Generative AI and formal verification, the skepticism was palpable. "Can large language models really understand the nuances of hardware verification?" colleagues would ask. Today, with our Best Paper Award from DVCon India 2024, we have a resounding answer: yes, they can—and they're transforming how we approach verification testplanning.

The Genesis of an Idea

The journey began at Intel, where I was leading a team of five engineers working on formal verification for datacenter products. We were consistently facing the same bottleneck: creating comprehensive formal verification testplans was taking weeks of expert effort. The process required:

  • Deep understanding of complex specifications
  • Exhaustive identification of all verification scenarios
  • Structured planning of checker implementation
  • Careful consideration of edge cases and corner conditions

This wasn't just time-consuming—it was a significant expertise barrier. Junior engineers struggled, and even experienced verifiers could miss critical scenarios.

The Breakthrough: Chain-of-Thought Prompt Engineering

The key insight came from understanding that testplan creation is fundamentally an information extraction task. We needed to convert unstructured specification documents into structured, actionable verification plans. This is exactly what modern LLMs excel at—when prompted correctly.

Our framework introduced a novel chain-of-thought prompting sequence that guides the AI model through the verification planning process step by step:

  1. Persona establishment - Setting the context as a formal verification expert
  2. Specification analysis - Systematic breakdown of design functionality
  3. Checker identification - Exhaustive listing of required properties
  4. Structured output - Formatted tabular testplans
  5. Completeness verification - Self-reflection on coverage
"The framework achieved 80-90% accuracy in generating formal verification testplans across designs of varying complexity, reducing testplanning time by 80%."

Real-World Impact: Three Case Studies

We validated our approach on three designs of increasing complexity:

1. Write Transaction Packer (Low Complexity)

A 659-flop design that packs partial writes into full-width transactions. Our GenAI-generated testplan achieved 99.74% mutation coverage when implemented. The AI correctly identified all seven critical checkers, including subtle behaviors like backpressure propagation and misaligned write handling.

2. ID Queue Manager (Medium Complexity)

An 18,816-flop queue management design with complex FIFO ordering requirements. Generated testplan achieved 93.05% formal core coverage, successfully capturing eleven distinct verification scenarios including masked search operations and non-destructive reads.

3. AXI4-Lite Crossbar (High Complexity)

A 44,096-flop fully-connected switch with 4.9 million gates. The AI-generated plan covered seventeen verification aspects and achieved 93.84% mutation coverage. This included sophisticated scenarios like decode error handling and runtime configuration changes.

The Evaluation Challenge

One of our key innovations was using formal coverage as an objective evaluation metric. Unlike subjective quality assessments, formal coverage provided quantifiable proof that our generated testplans were both accurate and exhaustive:

  • Observability coverage measured testplan exhaustiveness
  • Mutation coverage validated checker correctness
  • Formal core coverage assessed practical verification depth

This objective framework allowed us to iteratively refine our prompting strategy with confidence.

Lessons Learned

Through this journey, several critical insights emerged:

Prompt engineering is an art and a science. The difference between 60% and 90% accuracy often came down to subtle phrasing choices. Adding "please take care not to miss or repeat any checker" dramatically improved completeness.

Context matters immensely. Providing the right persona and constraints shaped outputs more than any other factor. Our "formal verification expert" persona produced fundamentally different (and better) results than generic verification requests.

Structured output requirements are essential. Specifying exact table formats with column descriptions eliminated ambiguity and made testplans immediately actionable.

Redundancy is manageable. While AI-generated plans sometimes included redundant checkers, careful review easily identified these—a far smaller effort than creating plans from scratch.

Looking Forward: The RAG Frontier

Our current work focuses on integrating Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) to further enhance accuracy. By grounding the AI in domain-specific knowledge bases—verification best practices, historical bug patterns, design-specific guidelines—we're pushing toward even higher accuracy and relevance.

The vision is clear: a future where verification engineers focus on complex reasoning and creative problem-solving, while AI handles the tedious, error-prone work of comprehensive testplan creation.
